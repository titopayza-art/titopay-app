Standards

How we work

You are in a building where young people live. Most of them are away from home for the first time, and many of them are funded by NSFAS or a bursary that depends on them staying in good standing. That is the whole reason the rules below exist.

The residents come first

  • Knock and wait before entering any room, every time, even when you have the key.
  • Never enter a room alone where a resident is present and asks you not to.
  • A resident's room, their post, their belongings and their business are private. What you see in a room stays in that room unless it is a safety matter.
  • Never ask a resident for money, favours, food or a lift, and never accept a gift that is offered to get something done faster.
  • Relationships with residents are not allowed. The power difference makes consent impossible to demonstrate, and it puts the resident and you at risk.

Safety is not negotiable

  • Report anything unsafe the day you see it — a loose rail, an exposed wire, a door that will not lock, a fire extinguisher past its date.
  • Never prop open a security door or lend out a master key.
  • Never work on electrical or gas equipment you are not qualified for.
  • Gender-based violence, assault, threats and harassment are reported to management immediately, and to the police where a crime has been committed. There is no informal route for these.

Honesty with money and property

  • Company stock, tools and supplies stay on site and are used for company work.
  • Every claim you send in is backed by the actual receipt.
  • Never take a payment from a resident in cash or into a personal account. All payments go through the bank details published on the website.

Confidentiality

You will see ID numbers, bank details, maintenance history and sometimes things residents have told us in confidence. This information is not yours to share, photograph, copy or discuss outside work — including on WhatsApp groups. This obligation does not end when your employment does.

Timekeeping and appearance

  • Be on site at the time on the roster, in the uniform or clothing agreed for your role.
  • If you cannot come in, phone your manager before your shift starts. A message sent after the shift began is not notice.
  • Alcohol and drugs do not belong on site or in your system while you are working.

When the rules are broken

Most matters start as a conversation with your manager. Where that is not enough the process is: a verbal warning, a written warning, a final written warning, and a disciplinary hearing where dismissal is possible. You may bring a fellow employee to any hearing, and you will be told the allegation in advance and given a chance to answer it. Theft, assault, gross dishonesty and bringing a resident into danger can go straight to a hearing.

Probation, plainly

New staff work a probation period of three months from your start date.

  • Probation is there so both sides can see whether the job fits. It is not a trial you are expected to fail.
  • You are paid your full agreed salary and you accrue leave from day one.
  • Your manager will sit down with you at roughly the halfway mark and again near the end, and tell you plainly how it is going.
  • If something is not working, you will be told what needs to change, given the training or guidance to change it, and given a fair chance to do so before any decision.
  • Probation can be extended once, in writing, with the reason stated, where more time would genuinely help.
  • Notice during probation is one week. After probation it is the period in your contract.

Nothing here removes your rights under the Labour Relations Act or the Basic Conditions of Employment Act. Your signed contract governs your employment.

Leave, in short

  • Annual leave is requested through the form, in advance, and confirmed in writing before you book anything.
  • Sick leave is phoned in before your shift and backed by a medical certificate where you are off for more than two consecutive days.
  • Family responsibility leave covers the birth of your child, or the illness or death of a close family member.
