Code of conduct and probation

What is expected of everyone who works at Ligcabho, what happens when it goes wrong, and how probation works. Read it once properly — it is part of your appointment.

Probation

Your first three months

Probation is not a trial you either survive or fail in silence. It is three months in which we train you, tell you plainly how you are doing, and give you the chance to fix anything that is not working before it becomes permanent.

How long it runs

Three months from your start date, unless your letter of appointment says otherwise. It can be extended once, by up to three further months, but only if we have told you in writing before the first three months end, explained exactly what still needs to improve, and given you the support to do it.

What we do during it

  • You are shown the job properly in your first week, not left to guess.
  • Your manager sits down with you at the end of month one and month two. Both conversations are written down and you get a copy.
  • If something is not right, you are told at the time and told what to do instead. Nothing should be raised at the end that you are hearing for the first time.

How it ends

  • Confirmed. You are appointed permanently, in writing, from that date.
  • Extended. In writing, with the reasons and what has to change.
  • Not confirmed. We meet with you first, you are told the reasons, and you get to respond before any decision is taken. If it still ends, you are given one week’s notice or pay in lieu, and your certificate of service.

Being on probation does not remove your rights. You are entitled to a fair reason and a fair process, the same as anyone else, and you may take an unfair dismissal to the CCMA.

What you get from day one

  • Your full agreed salary. Probation is not a lower rate.
  • Leave accrues from your first day, even though it is usually taken after month four.
  • UIF is deducted and paid over from the first payslip.
  • Notice: one week during the first six months.

Ask early

If you are unsure how something is done, ask before you do it. Nobody here has ever been in trouble for asking. People have been in trouble for guessing.

Code of conduct

The rules everyone works by

These apply to every person employed by Ligcabho Le’Africa Residences, on every site, on every shift, from the first day of probation onwards.

Being at work

Your shift times are in your letter of appointment. Be on the property, changed and ready, at the start of the shift, not arriving at it. If you are going to be late or cannot come in, phone your manager before the shift starts — a message after the fact is not notice. Three unexplained absences in a month is a disciplinary matter.

Students come first

Students live here. Speak to them the way you would want someone to speak to your own child at university, whatever the hour and whatever the provocation. If a student is rude to you, do not answer back: write it down and report it. We will deal with it.

Their rooms are their homes

Never enter an occupied room without knocking, announcing yourself and being let in, except in an emergency or a scheduled inspection the student has been told about. Never enter alone where you can avoid it.

Keys, access and security

Keys and access cards stay on you or in the safe, never with a student, a friend or a family member. Do not prop open a gate or a door. Do not let anyone in who cannot be signed in. Report a lost key the same day — not the next morning.

Money

Do not take cash from a student for anything, for any reason. Rent, deposits, sleepovers and damages are paid into the bank account and nowhere else. If a student offers you money, refuse it and report it.

Honesty

Theft, of anything, from anyone, at any value, ends employment and goes to the police. The same goes for falsifying a timesheet, a requisition or a maintenance record.

Alcohol and drugs

Do not come to work under the influence and do not use alcohol or drugs on the property. If you are on medication that affects how you work, tell your manager privately beforehand.

Harassment and discrimination

No one working or living here is to be harassed, sexually or otherwise, or treated differently because of race, sex, orientation, religion, nationality, disability or anything else. There is no informal version of this rule. Report it to any manager or the CEO, and it will be handled.

Other people’s information

You will see ID numbers, addresses, funding letters and family details. They are not yours to discuss, photograph, forward or post. That is the law (POPIA) as well as our rule.

The property

Look after the tools, the vehicles and the building. Report damage you cause — it is the hiding that gets people dismissed, not the accident.

Social media

Do not post photographs of students, their rooms or their documents. Do not speak for Ligcabho online. If the press or a parent asks you something official, refer them to the office.

Speaking up

If you see something wrong — a safety risk, a theft, a student being mistreated — report it. Nobody is disciplined for reporting something in good faith, even if it turns out to be a misunderstanding.

When it goes wrong

Discipline is corrective before it is final

The point of a warning is to fix the problem, not to build a file against you. Steps are normally followed in order, but serious misconduct can start at a hearing.

Informal discussion

Your manager raises it with you, tells you what needs to change, and writes a line in your file. Most things end here.

Verbal warning

Recorded, valid for three months.

First written warning

Recorded, valid for six months. You sign to say you received it — signing is not agreeing.

Final written warning

Recorded, valid for twelve months. Usually the last step before dismissal for that kind of conduct.

Disciplinary hearing

For serious misconduct, or when a final warning is already in place. You are told the charge in writing and given at least 48 hours to prepare. You may be represented by a fellow employee or a shop steward, you may state your case and ask questions, and you get the outcome in writing.

What counts as serious misconduct

Theft or fraud, assault, threatening anyone, sexual harassment, being drunk or high on duty, wilful damage, gross insubordination, sleeping on a security shift, handing over keys or access to an unauthorised person, or disclosing a student’s personal information. These can lead to dismissal for a first offence, after a hearing.

Your rights in any process

  • To be told the allegation in writing, and in a language you understand.
  • To reasonable time to prepare, and to be represented by a fellow employee or shop steward.
  • To state your case, bring witnesses and ask questions.
  • To a written outcome, and to appeal it to the CEO within five working days.
  • To refer an unresolved dispute to the CCMA.

Grievances

If something is being done to you that should not be, raise it with your manager first. If your manager is the problem, or nothing changes within five working days, send it in through the staff request form and mark it a grievance — it goes to the office, not to your manager, and you will be contacted.
