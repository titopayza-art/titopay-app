Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy

Version 2.0 Effective date: 13 August 2026 Company: TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, Reg. 2026/399418/07

This Privacy Policy explains how TitoPay collects, uses, stores, shares, protects and manages personal information across the TitoPay website and the TitoPay app, including the wallet, payments, ticketing, TitoKids, stokvel groups and business tools.

1. Who we are

TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, registration number 2026/399418/07, is a South African fintech company. TitoPay operates a digital wallet and payment app for individuals and businesses, together with event ticketing, family money tools (TitoKids), stokvel savings groups and business payment tools.

Our full supplier details, including our registered address and how to reach us, are set out under Company and supplier information. For any privacy question, use the legal enquiry form.

2. What this policy covers

This policy applies to everyone whose personal information TitoPay processes: website visitors and waitlist applicants, app customers, business and merchant accounts, staff added to a business account, event organisers and ticket buyers, stokvel group members, parents and children using TitoKids, partners, suppliers and job applicants.

Where a specific service has its own notice or terms, those apply in addition to this policy and not instead of it.

3. Personal information we collect

What we hold depends on which parts of TitoPay you use.

  • Account and identity. Your name, username, email address, mobile number, password and PIN (both stored only in hashed form, never in a readable form), profile photo, and for business accounts a business name and logo.
  • Verification and compliance. Verification status, review records, risk ratings and any documents you or your business submit for identity, business or event verification.
  • Wallet and transactions. Wallet balances and the full record of money in and out, including transfers, QR payments, top-ups, withdrawals, purchases of airtime, data, electricity and vouchers, references, fees and beneficiary details you save.
  • Ticketing. Tickets bought or received, ticket codes, order and refund records, attendee details supplied at purchase, scan records at an event entrance, and where an event is cashless the wristband or card tag linked to a ticket and the payments made with it. Event organisers additionally have event, venue, settlement and sales records.
  • TitoKids. A child's first and last name and date of birth, their wallet and its transaction history, spending limits, savings goals, money requests, and the identity of the parent or guardian and any co-parent who helps manage that child's wallet.
  • Stokvel. Group membership, contributions, payouts and withdrawals, group messages and meeting minutes.
  • Business and staff. Staff members linked to a business account, their role and permissions, the sales and transactions they process, products, stock records and payout account details.
  • Messages and support. The content of messages you send through TitoPay Chat to other people on TitoPay, support conversations, enquiries, complaints and anything you attach to them.
  • Technical and device. IP address, device and browser information, sign-in and session records, the devices signed in to your account, notification preferences, pages and screens used, and security and fraud monitoring logs.

TitoPay does not collect your biometric information, and does not ask for your banking passwords. Where the app uses your camera to scan a QR code or your phone's NFC reader to link an event wristband, that happens on your device with your permission and no image is stored by TitoPay.

4. How we collect it

  • Directly from you, when you register, complete a form, transact, contact support, add a child to TitoKids, join a stokvel, or communicate with TitoPay.
  • Automatically as you use the app and website, through server logs, session records, device information and security monitoring.
  • From a business you work for, where that business adds you as a staff member.
  • From a parent or guardian, where they add a child to TitoKids or invite a co-parent.
  • From payment, banking, verification and compliance partners, and from public, official or regulatory sources, where this is lawful and relevant to your account.

5. Why we process it

  • To open and run your account and wallet, and to carry out the payments and other instructions you give us.
  • To operate ticketing, TitoKids, stokvel groups and business tools, including settlements to organisers and businesses.
  • To verify identity, meet our obligations under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) and related law, and to carry out sanctions, risk and financial-crime checks.
  • To detect, prevent and investigate fraud, scams, money laundering, account takeover, unauthorised access and prohibited activity.
  • To provide support, resolve disputes, handle refunds and answer enquiries.
  • To keep the service secure, reliable and available, and to improve it.
  • To send you service notices about your account, and marketing where you have agreed to it or where the law otherwise allows it.
  • To comply with legal, regulatory, tax, accounting, audit and recordkeeping duties.

6. Our lawful grounds

TitoPay processes personal information in line with the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA). Depending on the situation our ground is your consent, the performance of our contract with you or steps taken before entering into it, compliance with a legal obligation, the protection of a legitimate interest of yours or of TitoPay, or another ground POPIA permits.

Our practices follow POPIA's eight conditions: accountability, processing limitation, purpose specification, further processing limitation, information quality, openness, security safeguards and data subject participation.

7. Children and TitoKids

TitoKids lets a parent or guardian open and manage a wallet for their child. To do that, TitoPay processes the child's personal information, which includes the child's name, date of birth, wallet balance and full transaction history, spending limits, savings goals and money requests.

POPIA treats a child's personal information as protected, and TitoPay processes it only on the basis of the consent of a competent person, meaning the child's parent or legal guardian. That consent is given by the adult who adds the child in the app, and it can be withdrawn at any time by removing the child, which closes the child's wallet and stops further processing.

A parent or guardian may invite another adult to help manage a child's wallet as a co-parent. Accepting that invitation gives that adult access to the child's wallet, balance, transaction history and requests. Only the parent who set up the wallet may invite or remove a co-parent. Both the invitation and the removal are recorded, and the invited person must accept before any access is given.

A parent or guardian may ask us at any time for a copy of the information we hold about their child, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it, subject to any record we are required by law to keep. Use the legal enquiry form.

Apart from TitoKids, TitoPay accounts are for adults. We do not knowingly open a wallet in a child's own name outside TitoKids, and if we learn that we have, we will act to correct it.

8. Who we share it with

TitoPay does not sell personal information.

We share it only where it is necessary, with: payment, banking and settlement partners who process a transaction; verification, compliance and fraud-prevention providers; hosting, cloud, email and communication providers who run our infrastructure; professional advisers, auditors and insurers; regulators, the Financial Intelligence Centre, law-enforcement agencies and dispute-resolution bodies where the law requires or permits it; and a party involved in a corporate transaction.

Some sharing is a normal part of the service you asked for. A merchant you pay sees the payment. An event organiser sees the tickets you hold for their event and, at the entrance, that your ticket was scanned. Other members of a stokvel group see your membership and your contributions to that group. A business you work for sees the sales you process for it.

Where a provider processes information on our behalf, we require confidentiality, security and purpose limitation from them in writing.

9. Information sent outside South Africa

Some of our technology, communication and security providers process information outside South Africa. Where that happens, we transfer it only on a basis POPIA allows, which in practice means a written agreement with the provider that gives the information a level of protection substantially similar to POPIA, or your consent, or because the transfer is necessary to perform our contract with you.

10. How long we keep it

We keep personal information only for as long as we need it, and then delete, destroy or de-identify it. Some periods are set by law rather than by us.

  • Identity, verification and transaction records: at least five years after the transaction or after the end of our relationship with you, as the Financial Intelligence Centre Act requires.
  • Accounting and tax records: five years, as tax and company law require.
  • Account and wallet records: for as long as the account is open, and then for the periods above.
  • Support conversations and complaints: normally three years after the matter is closed.
  • Chat messages between customers: normally two years, unless they are part of an investigation or a dispute.
  • Security, sign-in and fraud monitoring logs: normally twelve months, and longer where an investigation requires it.
  • Waitlist and marketing records: until you ask us to stop, and then only a suppression record so that we do not contact you again.
  • A child's TitoKids information: for as long as the child's wallet is open, then the periods above apply to the transaction record.

11. Automated decisions

TitoPay uses automated checks to protect customers and to meet its legal duties. These look at things like sign-in patterns, devices, transaction behaviour and risk signals, and they can delay or block a transaction, or restrict an account, while we review it.

POPIA gives you the right not to be subject to a decision made only by automated means that has a legal effect on you or affects you substantially, except where the law permits it or where suitable safeguards are in place. Where an automated check affects your account, you may ask for it to be reviewed by a person and you may put your side of the matter to us. Use the legal enquiry form or the in-app support chat.

12. Your rights

Subject to POPIA and other applicable law, you may ask us for a copy of the personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct information that is wrong or incomplete, ask us to delete or destroy information we no longer have a lawful reason to keep, object to processing in certain circumstances, withdraw a consent you gave us, and complain about how we have handled your information.

Some information we must keep even if you ask us to delete it, in particular the transaction and verification records the Financial Intelligence Centre Act requires.

To exercise a right, use the legal enquiry form and choose the relevant option. We may need to verify your identity first. We aim to respond within 30 days.

13. Marketing

We will send you service messages about your account, your transactions, your tickets and your security whenever we need to. These are part of the service and you cannot opt out of them while you hold an account.

Marketing is different. Where you are not yet a TitoPay customer, we send you electronic marketing only if you have agreed to receive it, for example by joining the waitlist or ticking the box on a form. Where you are already a customer, we may send you marketing about similar TitoPay products, and every such message carries a clear way to stop them.

You can withdraw your agreement at any time, in the app under notification settings, by using the unsubscribe link in any marketing email, or through the legal enquiry form. TitoPay will never ask you for a PIN, password, one-time PIN or verification code in a marketing message.

14. How we protect it

TitoPay treats security as part of the product. We use administrative, technical and organisational controls, including encrypted connections, hashed credentials, access control, session management, device records you can review and sign out, logging, monitoring and backups.

No online service can promise absolute security. Our Security Policy sets out what we do and what we ask of you.

15. If something goes wrong

If personal information is compromised, we will investigate and contain it, and we will notify the Information Regulator and the people affected as soon as reasonably possible, as POPIA section 22 requires. Where an incident also triggers a reporting duty under the Cybercrimes Act, we report it within the period that Act sets.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your personal information, please contact us first through the legal enquiry form so that we can put it right. You may also complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa, which can be reached at inforegulator.org.za or by email at complaints.IR@inforegulator.org.za.

16. Changes to this policy

We update this policy as the product and the law develop. Every version carries a version number and an effective date. Where a change materially affects your rights, we will tell you in the app or by email at least 30 days before it takes effect, unless a shorter period is required by law or needed for security.

POPIA Policy

POPIA Policy

Version 2.0 Effective date: 13 August 2026 Company: TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, Reg. 2026/399418/07

This POPIA Policy sets out how TitoPay meets its duties under the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013, as a responsible party operating a payment and wallet platform in South Africa. It should be read together with our Privacy Policy, Cookie Policy, Security Policy and Terms and Conditions.

1. Purpose and scope

This policy applies to every person whose personal information TitoPay processes, including customers, business accounts and their staff, event organisers and ticket buyers, stokvel members, parents and children using TitoKids, waitlist applicants, suppliers, partners and job applicants.

2. Responsible party

TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, registration number 2026/399418/07, is the responsible party for the personal information it processes through the TitoPay website, the TitoPay app and its business operations. Our address and contact details are set out under Company and supplier information.

3. Information Officer

TitoPay's Information Officer is the Chief Executive Officer of TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, who is the head of the private body for the purposes of POPIA and the Promotion of Access to Information Act. The Information Officer is responsible for encouraging compliance, dealing with requests, working with the Information Regulator and ensuring that this policy is followed. TitoPay's Information Officer is registered with the Information Regulator as the Regulations require.

The Information Officer can be reached through the legal enquiry form, choosing “POPIA request”, or by email at legal@titopay.co.za.

TitoPay's PAIA manual, which the Promotion of Access to Information Act requires every private body to keep, is available on request through the same channels.

4. Special personal information and children's information

TitoPay does process children's personal information, and does so only through TitoKids.

TitoKids allows a parent or guardian to open and manage a wallet for their child. In doing so TitoPay processes the child's name, date of birth, wallet balance, transaction history, spending limits, savings goals and money requests. Section 34 of POPIA prohibits the processing of a child's personal information unless a ground in section 35 applies. TitoPay relies on the consent of a competent person, being the child's parent or legal guardian, which is given in the app when the child is added and may be withdrawn by removing the child.

Where a parent invites a second adult to help manage the child's wallet as a co-parent, that access is given only on the instruction of the parent who set the wallet up, only after the invited adult accepts, and it can be withdrawn by that parent at any time.

Apart from this, TitoPay does not seek special personal information. TitoPay does not collect biometric information. Where a service in future requires special personal information, TitoPay will identify the lawful ground and say so before collecting it.

5. The eight processing conditions

  • Accountability. TitoPay maintains the policies, controls and review processes needed for lawful processing, under the Information Officer.
  • Processing limitation. TitoPay collects only what is adequate, relevant and not excessive for the purpose, lawfully and without unreasonable intrusion.
  • Purpose specification. Information is collected for the specific, lawful purposes set out in the Privacy Policy, and records are kept no longer than necessary or than the law requires.
  • Further processing limitation. Information is used further only where that use is compatible with the original purpose or where the law allows it.
  • Information quality. TitoPay takes reasonable steps to keep information accurate, complete and up to date, and customers can correct their own details in the app.
  • Openness. TitoPay maintains this policy and the Privacy Policy, tells people what it collects and why, and keeps the documentation POPIA requires.
  • Security safeguards. TitoPay secures information under its control with appropriate technical and organisational measures, and requires the same of its operators.
  • Data subject participation. People may access, correct and, where lawful, delete their personal information, and TitoPay responds to those requests.

6. Lawful grounds

TitoPay processes personal information on the ground of consent, the conclusion or performance of a contract, compliance with a legal obligation, the protection of a legitimate interest, or another ground POPIA permits. Where processing rests on consent, that consent may be withdrawn.

7. Operators

Other companies run parts of TitoPay for us: hosting, email delivery, payments, identity verification, fraud prevention and security monitoring.

Each of them handles personal information only on our written instruction, has to keep it confidential, has to protect it properly, and has to tell us straight away if there is reason to think somebody has got hold of it who should not have.

8. Direct marketing

Section 69 of POPIA governs electronic direct marketing. Where a person is not an existing TitoPay customer, TitoPay sends electronic direct marketing only with that person's consent, requested no more than once and in the manner the Act prescribes. Where a person is an existing customer, TitoPay may market similar TitoPay products to them, and every message gives a clear and free way to object.

Service messages about an account, a transaction, a ticket or a security event are not direct marketing and are sent as part of the service.

TitoPay will never ask for a PIN, password, one-time PIN, verification code or recovery information in a marketing message.

9. Cross-border transfers

Where personal information is sent outside South Africa, TitoPay relies on section 72 of POPIA. In practice this means a written agreement with the recipient that upholds principles substantially similar to POPIA's conditions, or the data subject's consent, or that the transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract.

10. Retention and destruction

Retention periods are set out in section 10 of the Privacy Policy. The Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) requires TitoPay to keep identity, verification and transaction records for at least five years. When a record is no longer needed and no law requires it, it is deleted, destroyed or de-identified so that it cannot be reconstructed.

11. Security safeguards

TitoPay works out what could realistically go wrong, puts safeguards in place against it, checks that those safeguards are actually working, and changes them as the risks change. Our Security Policy sets out what they are.

12. Security compromises

Where there are reasonable grounds to believe that personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, TitoPay will notify the Information Regulator and the affected data subjects as soon as reasonably possible after discovering the compromise, as section 22 of POPIA requires. Notification to data subjects will describe what happened, what information was involved, what we are doing about it and what the person can do to protect themselves. Notification may be delayed only where a public body responsible for criminal investigation tells us that it would impede an investigation.

Where the Cybercrimes Act, 2020 also applies, TitoPay reports the incident to the South African Police Service within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.

Report a security concern through the Security Centre in the app, the in-app support chat, or the legal enquiry form. Never include a password, PIN, one-time PIN or verification code in a report.

13. Automated decision making

TitoPay operates automated fraud, risk and compliance checks that can delay, block or restrict a transaction or an account. Section 71 of POPIA gives a data subject the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that has legal consequences or affects them substantially, unless the decision is necessary for a contract or is permitted by law and appropriate safeguards apply. TitoPay's safeguard is that a person reviews any such decision on request, and that the customer may make representations. Ask for a review through the in-app support chat or the legal enquiry form.

14. Data subject requests

Submit a request through the legal enquiry form, choosing “POPIA request”. Tell us your full name and contact details, which right you want to exercise, and enough detail for us to find the record. We may ask you to verify your identity before we act, so that we do not disclose your information to somebody else. We aim to respond within 30 days and will tell you if we need longer.

15. Complaints

Please raise a complaint with us first so that we can resolve it. If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa at inforegulator.org.za, by email at complaints.IR@inforegulator.org.za, or through the Regulator's prescribed complaint form.

16. Review of this policy

This policy is reviewed at least once a year and whenever the product, the law or our operations change materially. Each version carries a version number and an effective date.

Refund Policy

Refund Policy

Version 2.0 Effective date: 13 August 2026 Company: TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, Reg. 2026/399418/07

This Refund Policy explains when money comes back to you, how to ask for it, and how long it takes. It covers wallet transactions, purchases made through TitoPay, and event tickets.

1. The short version

If TitoPay took money and did not deliver what you paid for, we put it right. If a merchant or an event organiser owes you a refund, we help you get it and we move the money once it is agreed or once the law requires it. If you sent money to the wrong person using details you supplied, we will try to recover it but we cannot promise to.

2. Failed and duplicate transactions

If money left your wallet and the payment did not complete, or you were charged twice for the same thing, tell us and we will investigate. Where we confirm the error, we reverse or refund it. You do not need to prove anything beyond the transaction itself, because our own records show it.

Where a payment is still in progress with a bank, network operator or utility, we wait for that partner's outcome before reversing, so that you are not credited twice.

3. Money sent to the wrong person

A transfer you authorised to details you entered is not an error on our side, and we cannot simply take money back from another customer. We will contact the recipient and ask them to return it, and we will give you the records you need. Check the name shown to you before you confirm a payment, because that is the point at which a mistake is easiest to stop.

4. Purchases from merchants

Where you paid a merchant, the merchant decides whether goods or services qualify for a refund, under their own terms and under the Consumer Protection Act. Once the merchant agrees, or where the law requires the refund, we move the money and confirm it to you. We will give you the payment records you need to take the matter up.

5. Airtime, data, electricity and vouchers

These are delivered instantly by the relevant provider and generally cannot be reversed once delivered. Where delivery fails, we refund the purchase to your wallet.

6. Event tickets

  • Ask for a ticket refund in the app, under My Tickets, on the ticket concerned. It goes to the event organiser, and to TitoPay where the organiser's terms or the law require our involvement. You do not need to use the legal enquiry form.
  • The organiser's published refund terms apply. Many events are non-refundable by choice, and that must be stated before you buy.
  • If an event is cancelled, buyers are refunded. TitoPay refunds from the ticket money it is holding, and where that is not enough the organiser remains liable for the shortfall.
  • If an event is postponed, your ticket normally carries over to the new date. Where the new date does not suit you, the organiser's terms determine whether you get a refund.
  • A ticket that has already been scanned at the entrance has been used and is not refundable.
  • The 7-day cooling-off right in the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act does not apply to tickets for leisure events supplied on a specific date, because that Act excludes them.

7. Cashless event wristbands

A wristband or card at a cashless TitoPay event pays from your own wallet. There is no event float to load and nothing to reclaim afterwards, so there is no unspent balance to refund at the end of an event. A payment made with a wristband is refunded the same way as any other payment to that vendor.

If your wristband is lost or stolen, report it in the app immediately. We block it. Payments made before you reported it are treated under clause 8.

8. Transactions you did not authorise

Tell us immediately through the in-app support chat, the Security Centre, or the legal enquiry form. We may restrict the account while we investigate, and we will tell you when we do.

Where we conclude that a transaction was not authorised by you and did not result from you sharing your credentials or your device, we refund it. Where we conclude that it did result from that, we will explain why. Your rights under South African law are not affected by that conclusion, and you may take it further under clause 12.

It helps if you can give us the transaction reference, the date and time, the amount, the recipient, what happened, and any screenshots or messages. Never send us a password, PIN, one-time PIN or verification code.

9. Fees

Where we refund a transaction because of an error on our side, we refund the fee too. Where a refund happens for another reason, the fee for the original service may not be refundable, and we will tell you which applies before the refund is processed.

10. How long a refund takes

A refund back to a TitoPay wallet is normally immediate once approved. A refund to a bank account or a card depends on that institution and normally takes 3 to 10 business days. An organiser-approved ticket refund is processed once the organiser approves it, and cancellations are processed as soon as the cancellation is confirmed. Where an investigation is needed, we aim to conclude it within 20 business days and will tell you if it will take longer.

11. When we may decline

We may decline a refund request that is not supported by the transaction records, that is repeated after being answered, that appears fraudulent or abusive, or that concerns activity prohibited by our terms. We will tell you the reason unless the law prevents us.

12. If you are unhappy with our decision

Ask us to review it, through the in-app support chat or the legal enquiry form. If you are still unhappy, you may refer the matter to the National Financial Ombud Scheme South Africa at nfosa.co.za, to the National Consumer Commission, or to a South African court. This policy does not limit any right you have under the Consumer Protection Act, 2008, the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, 2002, or other applicable law.

Security Policy

Security Policy

Version 2.0 Effective date: 13 August 2026 Company: TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, Reg. 2026/399418/07

This Security Policy explains how TitoPay protects your money and your information, what we ask of you, and how to report a problem.

1. Our commitment

Security is part of the product, not a layer on top of it. TitoPay designs, builds, hosts and operates its service so that customer money and customer information are protected, and it reviews those controls as the product and the threats change.

2. How we protect your account

  • Passwords and PINs are stored only as cryptographic hashes. Nobody at TitoPay can read them.
  • All traffic between your device and TitoPay is encrypted in transit.
  • Sign-in from a new device triggers a verification step and a notification, so that you find out immediately if it was not you.
  • You can see every device signed in to your account and sign any of them out, at any time, from the app.
  • Sessions expire after a period of inactivity, and sensitive actions are confirmed separately.
  • Access to customer information by TitoPay staff is restricted by role, logged, and reviewed.

3. How we protect your money

  • Every balance is derived from a transaction ledger rather than a running total, so that a balance can always be reconciled against the movements that produced it.
  • Automated monitoring looks at sign-ins, devices, transaction patterns and risk signals, and can hold a transaction for review.
  • Limits apply to transaction size and frequency, and can be tightened where risk requires it.
  • Payment instructions show you the recipient and the fee before you confirm.

4. What we will never do

TitoPay will never ask you for your password, PIN, one-time PIN, verification code or recovery information. Not by phone, not by email, not by SMS, not by WhatsApp, not on social media, and not in the app. Anyone who asks you for these is trying to defraud you, whatever name they use.

TitoPay will never ask you to install remote-access software so that somebody can help you, and will never ask you to move your money to a “safe account”.

5. What we ask of you

  • Use a strong password that you do not use anywhere else, and protect your email account and phone number, because they can be used to recover access.
  • Never share your PIN, password, one-time PIN, verification code or device with anyone, including someone claiming to be from TitoPay.
  • Keep your phone locked and its software up to date.
  • Check the recipient and the amount before confirming a payment.
  • Report a lost or stolen phone or SIM immediately, and sign out that device from another one.
  • Treat urgency as a warning sign. A real TitoPay message never pressures you to act within seconds.

6. Business and organiser security

  • Give staff the least access they need, review it regularly, and remove it the day somebody leaves.
  • Protect your payout account details and confirm any change to them by a second channel.
  • Protect your event QR codes and scanner access, and review your sales and scan records.
  • Report unusual payment or settlement activity immediately.

7. Reporting a security problem

Report a security concern through the Security Centre in the app, the in-app support chat, or the legal enquiry form with the subject “Security concern”. Tell us what happened, when, and any transaction references, and attach screenshots if it is safe to do so. Never send a password, PIN, one-time PIN or verification code.

We acknowledge security reports and act on them. Where an incident affects you, we will contact you.

8. Responsible disclosure

If you are a security researcher and you believe you have found a vulnerability, please report it to us through the legal enquiry form or at security@titopay.co.za before disclosing it publicly.

While investigating, please do not access, modify or delete data that is not yours, do not degrade the service, do not use social engineering against our staff or customers, and do not run automated scanning that affects availability. We will acknowledge your report, keep you informed, and will not pursue action against researchers who follow this in good faith.

9. Incident response

When we become aware of a security incident, we contain it, investigate it, preserve the evidence, fix the cause and review what allowed it. Where personal information is compromised, we notify the Information Regulator and the people affected as soon as reasonably possible, as POPIA section 22 requires, and we report to the South African Police Service where the Cybercrimes Act requires it.

10. Availability

We build for reliability but no service is available every second. Maintenance, a partner outage, a security event or a technical fault can interrupt access. Where an interruption is planned, we tell you in advance where we can.

11. Changes to this policy

We update this policy as our controls, our products and the law develop. Each version carries a version number and an effective date.

Cookie Policy

Version 2.0 Effective date: 13 August 2026 Company: TitoPay (Pty) Ltd, Reg. 2026/399418/07

This Cookie Policy explains the cookies and similar technologies TitoPay uses on its website and in its app, and how you control them.

1. What cookies are

A cookie is a small file a website stores on your device. Similar technologies such as local storage and session storage do the same job. Some are essential to make a site work; others tell the site how it is being used, or support marketing.

2. The three categories TitoPay uses

These are the exact categories you will see in the privacy panel on this site.

  • Essential. These keep the site secure, keep forms and your session working, and remember the preferences you have set, including your cookie choice itself. The site cannot work without them, so they are always on and cannot be switched off.
  • Analytics. These tell us which pages are used and where people run into trouble, so that we can improve the site. They are off until you allow them.
  • Marketing. These help us measure and improve our campaigns and the relevance of what we show you. They are off until you allow them.

TitoPay does not sell information collected through cookies or browser storage.

3. Your choice, and how to change it

The first time you visit, a banner asks you to choose. Accept all switches on analytics and marketing. Essential only leaves both off. Manage cookies lets you switch each one on or off individually.

You can change your mind at any time using the Privacy settings button at the bottom of any page on this site. Your choice is remembered on that device and browser, so you will need to set it again on a different device, in a different browser, or after clearing your browser storage.

You can also block or delete cookies in your browser settings. Blocking essential cookies will stop parts of the site from working.

4. Cookies and POPIA

Cookie and storage identifiers can identify you, so POPIA applies to them. Analytics and marketing storage is used on the basis of your consent, which you can withdraw at any time as described above. Essential storage is used because it is necessary to provide the service you asked for. Our Privacy Policy explains your rights and how to exercise them.

5. The TitoPay app

The TitoPay app uses browser storage for the same essential purposes: keeping you signed in, holding your session securely, and remembering settings such as whether you have hidden your balance. Signing out of the app clears the personal data held on that device.

6. Changes

If we add or change a category, we update this policy and, where the change requires it, ask for your agreement again.
