THE QR PAYMENT SCREEN, AND ONE FAULT THAT WAS LOSING MONEY

   FOUR THINGS. THE SECOND IS THE SERIOUS ONE.

   1. THE OWNER OF A QR CODE CAN FINALLY BE NAMED.

      The review screen has always had a card for this and it has always read
      "Owner not confirmed" -- for every code, every customer, every time --
      because the app called GET /v1/qr/{id}/details and that endpoint was
      never built. The Security Tip screen tells people to read the verified
      recipient name before pressing Confirm. There was no name to read.

      The endpoint exists in build 43. The review now shows who owns the code:
      a registered business under its business name, a person under the name on
      their verified identity, with the TitoPay username beside it. It returns
      nothing else. No email address, no phone number, no wallet number, no
      balance, because a payment QR is shown to strangers by design.

      On an older API the caution card still appears, which remains the honest
      answer when nothing can be confirmed.

   2. A PAYMENT WITH NOBODY TO CREDIT TOOK THE MONEY ANYWAY.

      The recipient was credited only if their wallet could be resolved, and
      there was no other branch. A QR code whose owner had no wallet row
      therefore debited the payer in full, collected the fee, credited nobody,
      held nothing, and reported the payment as completed.

      Measured before the fix, on a R200 payment: R200.50 left the payer,
      R0.50 reached the revenue wallet, and R200.00 existed nowhere at all.

      Such a payment is now refused before anything moves, with a sentence that
      says plainly that nothing was taken. Only the wallet-to-wallet services
      reach that line, so nothing that legitimately pays out elsewhere is
      affected, and an ordinary QR payment settles exactly as it always did.

   3. PAYING YOUR OWN QR CODE IS REFUSED.

      It moved money in a circle and charged the fee for doing it. The review
      screen now says so before Confirm, and the API refuses it after.

   4. "UNABLE TO COMPLETE THE REQUEST" HAD A CAUSE, AND NOW IT NAMES ITSELF.

      Nothing in the codebase creates the TitoPay revenue wallet; it is
      provisioned once by scripts/ensure-revenue-wallet.js --create. Without it
      every fee-bearing wallet payment fails at Confirm while the fee preview on
      the same screen works perfectly, because only the settlement needs it.

      The error it raised was written for an operator, and the API correctly
      refuses to show an unauthored 5xx to a customer, so what reached the phone
      was the blanket "Unable to complete the request. Please try again."
      Nothing named the cause, on the phone or in the admin console.

      Two changes. The customer is now told that nothing was taken from their
      wallet, that this is a fault on TitoPay's side and not their account, and
      to contact Support -- without being shown TitoPay's internal wallet
      architecture. And the admin console's "revenue wallet exists" probe used
      to pass whenever the query RAN, so it reported OK on a database that had
      never had one. A probe that must return a row now fails when it does not.

      IF YOUR QR PAYMENTS ARE FAILING, THIS IS THE FIRST THING TO CHECK:

        node scripts/ensure-revenue-wallet.js            reports, changes nothing
        node scripts/ensure-revenue-wallet.js --create   provisions it

   Proven in verification/qr-payment-integrity-live.js over the real HTTP API:
   the owner is named and nothing private leaks, a payment with no recipient is
   refused with the payer's balance identical before and after, a self-payment
   is refused and costs nothing, an ordinary payment still settles at payer
   -R200.50 and merchant +R200.00, and a missing revenue wallet produces a
   sentence a person can act on. 8 checks.

   Also verified unchanged: 13/13 money path checks, including "every cent
   debited is credited somewhere".


A BUSINESS CAN START FROM ITS COMPANY REGISTRATION NUMBER

   Limits & Verification offered a South African ID, a passport and "other
   approved identity document". A business owner holding a CIPC certificate and
   nothing else had no option on that screen at all.

   The screen that DOES take a registration number, Business Verification, then
   refused to show its form until the owner had already verified themselves with
   an identity document. So the two screens pointed at each other and the only
   way out was the ID number, which is exactly what it looked like from inside.

   TWO CHANGES, AND THE SECOND IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS.

   1. A FOURTH OPTION, ON BUSINESS ACCOUNTS ONLY.

      Identity document now reads: South African ID, Passport, Company
      registration number, Other approved identity document. Choosing the third
      swaps the lower half of the form to the business name, the type of
      business and the registration number, and the button becomes Add this
      business. A personal account still sees three options and never sees a
      company field.

      A sole proprietor, partnership or informal trader has no registration
      number and is still never asked for one.

   2. THE BUSINESS CAN BE RECORDED BEFORE THE PERSON IS VERIFIED.

      Capturing an entity is not verifying it. Writing down a name and a
      registration number proves nothing on its own, so there was nothing to
      protect by refusing it, and refusing it is what created the loop.

      WHAT DID NOT CHANGE, AND MUST NOT: a company registration number does not
      verify a person and does not move a limit. It says a company is on a
      register anybody can search. It says nothing about who is holding the
      phone. The business is recorded as unverified and stays that way, and it
      cannot be submitted for verification until a person is verified behind it.

   Proven in verification/business-registration-option.js, driven in a real
   browser: a business account sees four options and a personal account sees
   three, the form swaps, the business is written with its registration number,
   the person is STILL unverified afterwards, the business is unverified, the
   submit is refused with a reason a person can read, and a sole proprietor is
   accepted with no number at all. 9 checks.

   A test fails the build if that branch is ever pointed at the personal
   verification endpoint, or if it ever tells a customer their identity is
   verified.


THE EVENT POSTER: VISIBLE ON THE SHARED LINK, RIGHT SHAPE, AND CHANGEABLE

   THREE THINGS. THE FIRST IS THE ONE A CUSTOMER SEES FIRST.

   0. THE SHARED EVENT LINK SHOWED NO POSTER AT ALL.

      One event has two screens in the app, and only one of them is reached by
      tapping a card:

        the in-app sheet, opened from Events, which drew the poster
        the standalone screen a shared titopay.co.za/events/<name> link opens,
          which never drew it

      The second one is the screen that matters most, because it is what an
      organiser sends to their audience and what someone without the app sees.
      It opened on a heading and a wall of text, with the poster nowhere on it.

      The poster was in the payload the whole time. That screen simply never
      rendered it. It now shows it at the top, at the same 16:9 the event card
      uses, so the link and the card agree. An event with no poster is unchanged.

      Nothing else on that screen moved: the tickets, the price, the venue, the
      organiser links and the buy flow are all exactly where they were.

   1. THE DIMENSIONS WE ASKED FOR WERE THE WRONG ONES.

      The Create Event form asked for portrait 1080 x 1350 (4:5). The event
      card renders the poster at 16:9 with background-size: cover. So a poster
      made to our own spec had roughly two thirds of its height cropped away,
      keeping only a band across the middle. On a text-heavy poster the result
      was unreadable, with the heading clipped off the top.

      The form now asks for landscape 1920 x 1080 (16:9), which is the shape
      the card actually draws, and says plainly what happens to a portrait
      image if one is used anyway. Nothing about the card changed, so every
      poster already uploaded looks exactly as it does today.

   2. THE ORGANISER CAN REPLACE THE POSTER ON A LIVE EVENT.

      Approved events could not be edited in the app at all, which meant a
      wrong poster on a selling event could only be fixed by an admin. The
      poster is now the one exception: Business Ticketing > Events shows the
      current poster with an Add or Replace poster control, and the change is
      live at once.

      Everything else on an approved event still goes through the change
      request an admin reviews. The date, the venue, the price and the refund
      terms are what a buyer decided on; the poster is the shop window.

      Every replacement is written to that event's audit trail with who did it
      and when. A cancelled or finished event refuses, with a reason a person
      can read. Another business cannot touch it.

   Proven in verification/event-poster-replace-live.js against a real approved
   event selling paid tickets: the ordinary edit path still refuses it, the
   poster alone changes, the event stays approved with nothing else moved, the
   audit row is written, an empty upload is refused rather than wiping the
   poster, and another business is turned away. 8 checks.

   NEW: verification/event-poster-shared-link.js opens a real shared link in a
   real browser and measures what is on the page. Run against the v416 bundle it
   fails on "no poster on the shared-link screen"; against this one it passes 7,
   including that the browser decoded the image and drew it at 16:9.


THE SECURITY TIP SCREEN IS BACK AFTER SIGN-IN

   THIS IS A FAULT v413 INTRODUCED, AND THIS BUILD FIXES IT.

   The back-gesture work in v413 made every popstate close the top layer. But
   setting location.hash ALSO fires popstate, and signing in does exactly that
   with a sheet still open: it sets the route to dashboard, closes the sign-in
   sheet, then opens the Security Tip screen. The close ran a moment later, by
   which time the sheet on screen was the Security Tip screen rather than the
   sign-in sheet the gesture was aimed at, so it closed the wrong one.

   The result was that the Security Tip screen stopped appearing after sign-in
   in v413 and v414. Nothing errored, nothing was logged, and the screen itself
   was never broken: it opened and was removed a fraction of a second later.

   A popstate that moved the route is now recognised for what it is and closes
   nothing, and any close is checked against the exact sheet that was on screen
   when the gesture arrived.

   NEW: verification/pwa-sign-in-journey.spec.js drives the real journey -- tap
   Sign in, type, press the button -- and watches for eight seconds. Run against
   the v414 bundle it fails five checks; against this one it passes twelve. The
   original testing called showSecurityTipModal() directly, which worked
   perfectly while the journey was broken, and that is exactly why this was
   missed.

   Every back-gesture behaviour from v413 still holds: 19 native feel checks
   pass, including closing a sheet, the Pay hub, and a dialog over a sheet.


THE BUSINESS NOTE THAT v416 ADDED IS NOW THE OPTION ITSELF

   v416 added one sentence to "Verify your identity" on a business account,
   telling the owner that their registration number belonged on a different
   screen. That was honest, and it was not enough: the screen it pointed at
   would not show its form until the owner had already verified themselves,
   so the sentence sent people to a door that was locked from the other side.

   The sentence is replaced by the fourth option described at the top of these
   notes. A business owner can now act on that screen rather than be redirected
   from it. Personal accounts see no change at all.


EVERYTHING BELOW ARRIVED IN v413, AND SHIPS WITH THIS UPLOAD

THE APP BEHAVES LIKE AN APP

Everything below was found by walking the real bundle across the real screens
on two phone sizes and measuring it, not by reading the source. Each item is
something a person can see on a phone.


1. THE BACK GESTURE NOW CLOSES WHAT IS ON TOP.

   This is the one that mattered. The sheets, the Pay hub and the confirm
   dialog lived outside the browser's history, so the system Back gesture
   skipped straight past them. On Android, pressing Back with a sheet open left
   the app. On iPhone, swiping in from the edge took the whole screen away with
   a sheet still sitting over it. No installed app behaves that way, and it is
   the loudest difference between an app and a page saved to a home screen.

   Back now peels one layer at a time, top layer first:

     a confirm dialog    is cancelled, and the sheet under it stays open
     the Pay hub         closes, and the screen under it does not move
     a sheet             closes, and the screen under it does not move
     a sheet within a    returns to the sheet that opened it, the way the
       sheet             back arrow in its corner already did
     nothing on top      an ordinary move between screens, as before

   Closing a sheet by hand (the X, the backdrop, Escape) gives the history
   entry back, so the very next Back is a real navigation rather than a press
   that appears to do nothing.

   Every existing way of closing a sheet still works exactly as it did.


2. EVERY CONTROL ANSWERS A TAP.

   The app deliberately suppresses the grey flash iOS paints over a tapped
   control, because that flash is the most website-looking thing a web app
   does. The cost is that any control with no pressed state of its own gives no
   feedback at all, and a tap that shows nothing reads as a miss.

   A walk of the screens found three that had no answer: the Personal /
   Business segment on the landing, the statement period chips, and the small
   dismiss button on the download prompt. All three now use the same press the
   tiles and buttons already use, and all three respect reduced motion.


3. THE FIRST CONTROL A NEW CUSTOMER TOUCHES IS BIG ENOUGH.

   On a short screen the Personal / Business segment was 40px tall, under the
   44px both platforms ask for. Measured before it was changed: at 44px the
   landing still fits with no scrolling on 360x640, 375x667, 360x740, 390x664,
   412x732 and 414x736.


4. THE LAUNCH FLASH IS GONE, ON EVERY iPHONE.

   iOS shows a white rectangle between tapping the icon and the app's first
   paint unless it is given a startup image whose pixel size matches the device
   EXACTLY. The app declared one such image, for one phone — and that file had
   been truncated when it was written: the top 660 rows of 2532 were all that
   was ever there, with no end marker, so it would not decode. Every device,
   including the one it was cut for, got the flash the file existed to prevent.

   There are now eleven, covering every iPhone from the SE to the 16 Pro Max,
   each drawn with the same gradient and wordmark the app paints on its own
   first frame, so the handover is invisible. They are not precached, so first
   load is no slower. A test reads every one of them off disk and fails the
   build if any is truncated or is not the size it claims.

   Regenerate them with verification/make-splash-screens.py.
